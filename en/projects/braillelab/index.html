BrailleLab Ecuador: learning to build open Braille technology.

An AEMCiCD program for hands-on learning and technology development. Students design, build and document electronic refreshable Braille prototypes, with attention to repairability and the involvement of people with visual impairments.

Explore the purposeExplore BrailleTech Challenge
Why we support it

Technical learning connected to an accessibility need

Braille represents characters through combinations of raised dots. A refreshable cell changes the position of those dots electronically. Building a prototype requires combining mechanics, electronics and programming, as well as understanding how a person explores the dots through touch.

BrailleLab turns this challenge into a collaborative learning experience. Teams should understand and document their design decisions so that others can review, repair or continue their work. User involvement guides evaluation of the solutions.

The program contributes to AEMCiCD's academic and social purposes by connecting scientific learning, student cooperation and technology with a social purpose. Open design aims to share knowledge and support the continued development of prototypes.

Who it is for

Students and teams

People interested in learning through prototype design. The work combines electronics, programming, mechanical design, documentation and collaboration. Each edition sets its participation requirements and deliverables.

Users and organizations

People with visual impairments and organizations that can contribute their experience to identifying needs and validation. Their participation helps assess whether technical decisions fit the intended use.

Mentors and collaborators

Faculty, professionals and organizations that can support learning, technical review or materials. The scope of each collaboration is coordinated with the program team.

2027 edition · In preparation

BrailleTech Challenge Ecuador

The 2027 edition is co-organized with the IEEE Yachay Tech Student Branch. The challenge is to design and build an electronically controlled, documented six-dot refreshable Braille cell. The planned process combines training, prototyping and a final presentation.

Learning
Connect accessibility needs with design, manufacturing and programming decisions.
Technical work
Build and test prototypes, record results and explain the limitations found.
Documentation
Describe components, assembly and operation so that others can review and continue the work.
Continuity
Incorporate feedback and retain the information needed to repair or improve the design.

The challenge is an educational and development initiative. Prototypes are presented according to their progress; outreach and validation targets describe planned work, not results already achieved.

2027 technical campaign

What support is requested for

The campaign has a technical funding target of USD 6,500 and an initial milestone of USD 1,000. Planned resources support kits, components, materials and prototyping for the 10 teams advancing to that stage. The bootcamp and Demo Day have a separate budget.

AEMCiCD coordinates institutional resources. The BrailleLab website publishes authorized contribution instructions, the form for reporting transfers and financial updates. Progress distinguishes confirmed income, expenses, commitments and in-kind contributions; a fundraising target is not money already received.
